Why "it depends" isn't a real quote

The scope that never ends

Ask most agencies what something costs, and you'll get "it depends on scope" — which sounds reasonable until you notice the scope conversation itself never actually ends. Every answer opens three more questions, and somehow the number keeps moving.

Who that actually protects

A vague quote isn't neutral — it protects whoever wrote it. It leaves room to charge more once you're already committed, and it makes comparing two agencies almost impossible, since neither number means the same thing.

What a real number requires

To give a number you can actually trust, we have to understand what you need first. That's the whole reason the free call exists — not to sell you, but to get specific enough that the price we give afterward is one we'll actually stand behind.

What it costs us to work this way

Sometimes we underestimate something and absorb the difference ourselves, because that's the deal we made. Sometimes a project turns out simpler than expected, and the client pays less than they braced for. Either way, the number we gave is the number that holds.

Why we still quote custom work separately from the package

Our local-growth package works because the scope is fixed — that's what makes a published starting price possible. Custom work doesn't have a fixed scope by definition, so it gets a real conversation and a real number, not a guess dressed up as a policy.
