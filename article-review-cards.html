What an NFC review card actually does

The gap between happy and reviewed

Most customers who have a great experience never leave a review — not because they didn't like it, but because leaving one takes just enough effort that "I'll do it later" quietly becomes "I forgot." That gap between happy and reviewed is where most businesses lose their best, cheapest marketing asset.

What the card actually does

An NFC card removes the friction, not the decision. Someone taps their phone on a card at the counter or the table, and their phone opens straight to a pre-filled review screen — no searching for your business name, no typing, nothing to download. The choice to leave a review was already made; the card just removes the fifteen small steps between "yes" and "done."

Why this compounds instead of just adding up

Reviews don't just sit there — they change what happens next. A business with recent, frequent reviews ranks higher in local search, and looks more trustworthy to the next person deciding between you and a competitor with three reviews from two years ago. Each review makes the next customer's decision a little easier, which is why review count and freshness compound rather than simply accumulate.

Where it actually gets used

At checkout, on a table, at reception, next to a card reader — anywhere a happy moment naturally ends. It works best right after the good part, not in a follow-up email three days later when the feeling has already faded.

What it isn't

It's not a way to filter out bad reviews or game the system — a genuinely bad experience still gets tapped and reviewed honestly. It's a way to make sure your real average actually shows up, instead of only your most frustrated customers bothering to speak up while everyone who was happy stays silent.
